# Cali Interview

Date: 2026-11-16

Purpose: Record how the owner actually found salsa dancing in Cali, to build the Cali answer key and reusable discovery strategies.

## Q&A

### Round 1

**Questions**

1. When did you go to Cali, how long did you stay, and was salsa the reason you went?
2. How did you find the very first place you danced, and what did you search for or who told you?
3. What was that first place like, and what led you to the next spot after it?
4. Where were you staying, and did that place or its people point you anywhere for salsa?

**Answers (verbatim)**

Yes, Salsa was the reason I went. I stayed for a little more than two months. How did I find the first place? I stayed in a hostel called La Circle Sal, and I kind of got things by word of mouth. Then I found out about a school called Salsa Porta, and from there, I kind of started taking classes. I'm always around people, and then you just start to find the different events. I went during a big salsa festival. A lot of people there didn't really dance salsa much. I did like taking classes and everything, but they're just there to have fun and be around the stuff. The hostel events were pretty open to everybody and not really difficult, just kind of an activity for everyone, even if you've never danced salsa before.

I sort of want to reverse engineer that. Salsa Pura was a good spot, and there are some other dance schools too, which I'm not really aware of.

I also like Puerto Escondido. It is another place I want to try to reverse engineer, like what these dance schools will get, like their Instagrams. I think from their Instagrams, you can learn a lot about their events. Maybe our app can scan through certain Instagram accounts that we find for different places and rate them to see how relevant they are and things like that.

Also, there are just certain websites. I'll let you know, but for Puerto Escondido, for example, there's a website I found, which was my initial entrance to the salsa event. Eventually, I came across this salsa school. I forgot the name of it now, or timba, whatever.

Anyway, I want to try to reverse engineer. Those are the answers to your question.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- School name given as both "Salsa Porta" and "Salsa Pura". Asked to confirm.
- Hostel name "La Circle Sal" may be a speech-to-text spelling. Asked to confirm.
- Puerto Escondido (Mexico) came up. It gets its own interview file after Cali, so the Cali notes stay clean.
- Strategy ideas from the owner: hostel word of mouth → school → classes → events; school Instagram accounts as an event source, scored for relevance; local salsa websites as a first way in.

### Round 2

**Questions**

1. Was the school "Salsa Pura" or "Salsa Porta", and how do you spell the hostel's name?
2. At the hostel, who first told you about salsa: staff, other guests, or a flyer or group chat? What did they tell you?
3. Once you were taking classes at the school, how did you hear about events: from teachers, classmates, Instagram, or WhatsApp groups?
4. Which places do you remember dancing at in Cali, and which nights did you go to each one?

**Answers (verbatim)**

For number one, the school was the first option. You said Salsa Pura. How do you spell the hostel's name? It was LA SURSURCUSAL, I think, but it was just one of many hostels at this time. This is the salsa capital of the world, so these different hostels are all going to cater, but that's the one that I went to.

There's also Viajero. I'm sure they had events, but I never went there. Las Sorcosales is the place I stayed at. Once I was taking classes at the school, how did you hear about the events? I heard about the events through different group chats and even on the Instagram accounts of these places. You follow them, and you can see different events they're having. Word of mouth also.

Which places do I remember dancing in Cali? I remember the socials at Salsa Pura. Those were cool, and those were nice. I guess La Topa, but in Cali, honestly, it was a different vibe. This is one thing: you couldn't just go there and dance. It was more of a whole different style of salsa, and it was kind of intimidating to just go in there and dance. In the normal style of salsa, I think people are used to dancing. It's a whole different style.

As opposed to places like Cusco, you start finding events, and the events seem pretty chill to just go to, dance, and have fun. It's not like you're in a whole new salsa world, or the same for Puerto Escondido.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- School confirmed: Salsa Pura.
- Hostel spelled "LA SURSURCUSAL" and "Las Sorcosales". The real name is still unconfirmed and should be checked in the pilot. It may be "La Sucursal".
- Viajero: another hostel the owner believes had salsa events but never went to. Unverified.
- "La Topa": the full venue name is unconfirmed and should be checked in the pilot.
- Event sources the owner used: group chats, venue and school Instagram accounts (follow them to see events), and word of mouth.
- Vibe: Salsa Pura's socials were "cool" and "nice". La Topa felt intimidating because it was a different style, and you couldn't just walk in and dance.
- Cusco and Puerto Escondido felt easier to just show up and dance. Cusco is another possible city interview later.
- Data-model idea: record the style danced at each venue and whether a visitor can walk in and dance.

### Round 3

**Questions**

1. Which night were the Salsa Pura socials, and who went: mostly locals or tourists, beginners or advanced? Did they cost anything?
2. How did you get into the group chats: did the school add you, a classmate, or the hostel? Were they WhatsApp groups?
3. What was the big festival called, and did any of its events become places you kept going to afterward?
4. Did anything you tried in Cali turn out to be a dead end, like a place that was only salsa music with no dancing?

**Answers (verbatim)**

I don't remember which nights this also appeared on socials, but I'm sure I could find that from the Instagram pages.

How did you get into the groups? I got into the groups by, I think, usually just signing up for classes, and I'll be like, "Hey, we'll add you to the group now." Also, sometimes cities have chats, I think. Just in my home city, some people told me about a Medellin chat. It's kind of just random.

Also, in the DMV, there's a website called migente.com.

Yeah, the festival was big. Did any of those events become places you kept up with? The events were more like a car parade, so for the festival, anything trying to turn out to be dead at the end? I don't think anything in Cali was dead at the end, but certain events were. I guess just different vibes. Some were kind of more friendly to everyone, and some were for just hardcore professional dancers. Similar for locals, just different kinds of vibes

**Notes (interviewer)**

- The owner doesn't remember which night the Salsa Pura socials were. They expect the school's Instagram to show it, so the pilot should check there.
- Group chats: the owner was usually added after signing up for classes. Some cities also have city-wide chats that you hear about from people, sometimes even back home. Someone in the owner's home city told them about a Medellín chat.
- migente.com: a salsa events website for the DMV (the owner's home area). It's another city-level events site, like Salsavida. Unverified.
- Festival: the events were "more like a car parade". The name and month are still unknown.
- The owner hit no dead ends in Cali. Events differed by vibe: some were friendly to everyone, some were for hardcore or professional dancers, and some were mostly locals.
- Data-model idea: record a level or crowd vibe for each event (open to everyone vs. advanced or professional, locals vs. tourists).

### Round 4

**Questions**

1. Which month or year were you in Cali? That will help pin down the festival.
2. Besides Salsa Pura and La Topa, can you name any other places, even partly, like a bar, park, or a night at another school?
3. On the friendly vs. professional split, how could you tell which kind a place was before you walked in?
4. Do you remember Salsa Pura's Instagram name, or any other account that was useful in Cali?

**Answers (verbatim)**

I was in Cali from mid-December to, I think, maybe the end of February. It's the biggest salsa festival that Cali has, so whatever. It's during Christmas time, sort of. I think it's called Feria.

Besides Salsapira and Latopo, can you name other places? I don't remember. There's one place called Diablo. That was another clubbish place to dance, but it was more local and cool. What else? I think I just remember dancing at Salsapira. Pure and Salsa Pure had some socials.

Latopo is kind of what I did when I first got there, but I didn't go there as much. I would just rather do socials at the school after I found out about the school. Before I found out about Salsa Pure, I didn't do Salsa Pure and started taking classes. After the whole festival was over, during the festival, I would just be on the street dancing salsa with random people that didn't really take classes and everything. It was cool.

Salsa Pura's Instagram page should be very easy to find, but no, I don't remember it off the top of my head. You start following different people and getting information while you're in Cali, but I don't remember any Instagram handles off the top of my head. Salsa Pura is definitely a solid one to follow to have a good entry point.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- Dates: mid-December to about the end of February. The year wasn't given.
- Festival: "Feria", around Christmas. It's probably the Feria de Cali, but that's unverified.
- "Salsapira", "Salsa Pure" and "Pure" all mean Salsa Pura. "Latopo" means La Topa. Both look like speech-to-text spellings.
- Diablo: a club-style place to dance that was "more local and cool". Unverified.
- Order of events: La Topa came first, on arrival. During the festival the owner danced in the street with people who hadn't taken classes. Later they found Salsa Pura and switched to classes and the school's socials. The order around the festival is a little unclear in the answer.
- Q3 (how to tell a place's vibe before walking in) wasn't answered.
- Strategy: Salsa Pura's Instagram is the best way into Cali. Following people there leads to more accounts and events.

### Round 5

**Questions**

1. Before you walked in, how could you tell whether a place was friendly to everyone or for hardcore dancers? For example from Instagram posts, reviews, or what people told you.

**Answers (verbatim)**

Before you walked in, how could you tell? I guess it was just kind of... I don't know. It just seemed like people kind of knew each other more. If you weren't really confident about dancing, it's kind of difficult to just jump in there at this place for some reason. I don't know. It just felt a lot more difficult to just jump in here, and it just felt like the bar was higher for some reason.

I kind of just heard about it by word of mouth. It wasn't Lito, it was some other place. I don't even remember the name or what it was called at all. I probably couldn't even find it again unless I asked about it if I went back.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- The owner couldn't tell a place's vibe before going in; they only felt it once there. Signs inside: people seemed to know each other, and the bar felt higher for someone not confident about dancing.
- The intimidating place was found by word of mouth. The name is forgotten and not findable without asking locals. "Lito" is unclear: it may be a mishearing of La Topa or another venue. Ask later if needed.
- Takeaway: whether a place is open to newcomers is hard to find in advance. The pilot should test whether reviews or Instagram show it ("regulars", "advanced", "professional").
