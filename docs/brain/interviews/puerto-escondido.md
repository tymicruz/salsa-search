# Puerto Escondido Interview

Date: 2026-11-16

Purpose: Record how the owner actually found salsa dancing in Puerto Escondido, to build its answer key and reusable discovery strategies.

## Q&A

### Notes the owner volunteered (verbatim, given during the Cali interview)

URL: https://www.salsavida.com/guides/mexico/oaxaca/puerto-escondido/

Really quick, before I answer these next questions, I just want to give you this URL. This is a website that usually comes up if you just type salsa in a certain city. It'll pop up, and this was my first entry to find different salsa events, but a lot of these weren't exactly what you're looking for.

For example, on Tuesday, I don't think that was really... maybe there were only a few people there. The Salsa Nights at PMA on Tuesday, it looks like those were good. Congo was definitely not really a salsa event. I found out it was more of just a club. They played two salsa songs during this hour of salsa. Anyway, it was definitely not an event anybody would really want to go to for strictly salsa.

Mandala was one of the best ones, and Hotel Nectar is there too. Yes, a lot of the events are actually here, but some of these are events that would be better if they had sort of ratings, to see which ones people would want. Salsa Vida's one. I'm going to check to see what I see when I look up Cusco.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- Salsavida.com's city guide was the owner's first way in. It lists many events, but some of them aren't really for dancing, and it has no ratings.
- Owner's memories, all unverified:
  - Salsa Nights at PMA (Tuesday): "looks like those were good". It's unclear whether this was the Tuesday event with only a few people.
  - Congo: a club, not a salsa event. It played about two salsa songs during its "salsa hour".
  - Mandala: "one of the best ones".
  - Hotel Nectar: listed, with no vibe given.
- Lesson: a "salsa hour" listing doesn't mean people partner-dance there. This is the music-vs-social-dancing problem the data model has to handle.
- A full Puerto Escondido interview comes after Cali.

### Round 1

**Questions**

1. When were you there, and for how long?
2. Mandala was one of the best: what made it good? Who went (locals or tourists), how good were the dancers, and which night was it?
3. Do you remember anything about the salsa school you found later, like what area it was in, whether it was salsa or timba, or how you found it?
4. Was there dancing anywhere outside those listings, like on the beach, at a hostel, or at a park?

**Answers (verbatim)**

Where were you there? Now, Porto Santo Domingo. I was in Porto for 5 months. Mandala was one of the best. What made it good? It was just good vibes. It was consistent. It was just a good time every time. I just really liked dancing there. People from my dancing school hosted it, so we would all go there after practice on Thursdays, and it was always a good time.

Another place I liked a lot was Nectar, but it was a whole different vibe. It was just a different vibe, but it was also really fun. Those were the two best socials, I think, maybe the only two. There was also Payame, but I don't know. Sometimes people weren't there, and I guess, for the part of the town it was in, it was in a nice part of town. For whatever reason, it just wasn't as good. It was still good, but it wasn't always as good as Nectar and Mandala were more consistently good.

I guess for the vibes, Mandala was good because it was hosted by my school, so you kind of always had new people. Nectar, it's just a nice environment, and there were still a lot of the same people dancing.

Do you remember anything about Salsa School?

Yeah, the Salsa School, I was there every month for 4 months straight. Out of the 5 months I was there, once I found Timba, it was my favorite thing because you have community and everything. It was one of the best things. I feel like from this Salsa School, I just met so many people in Puerto. You're in a lot of different communities that overlap with the salsa community, and I feel like the heart of the salsa community was almost pretty much at Timba. A good part of the people that were actually taking classes were at Timba. That was probably the biggest school in the city that I was aware of.

Was there dancing anywhere else outside of those listings?

Not sure.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- "Porto Santo Domingo" and "Porto" mean Puerto Escondido (speech-to-text).
- Stay: 5 months. Dates not given yet.
- School: "Timba". The owner attended for 4 months straight and calls it "the heart of the salsa community" and "probably the biggest school in the city". Whether "Timba" is the school's name or the style it teaches is unconfirmed.
- Mandala: a social hosted by people from the school on Thursdays, after practice. "Good vibes", consistent, "a good time every time", with new people always coming. Unverified.
- Nectar (Hotel Nectar): "a whole different vibe" but really fun, a nice environment, a lot of the same people. Night not given. Unverified.
- "Payame": in a nice part of town, sometimes empty, less consistent. It may be the same as "PMA" (Salsa Nights at PMA, Tuesday) from the earlier notes. Unconfirmed.
- The owner thinks Mandala and Nectar were the two best socials, maybe the only two. They don't know of any dancing outside the listings.
- Strategy: the main school hosts the best social, so finding the main school leads to the main social (supports move 4).

### Round 2

**Questions**

1. Is "Timba" the school's actual name, or the style it teaches?
2. Is "Payame" the same as the "Salsa Nights at PMA" on Tuesday from Salsavida?
3. Which night was Nectar, and roughly which months were you there?
4. How did you find Timba: Salsavida, Google Maps, Instagram, or someone telling you?

**Answers (verbatim)**

Timba taught. A lot of schools teach salsa and bachata, obviously, and there were some days we learned different things, like cha cha. It just depends. We do tcha sometimes and on to salsa. It was a proper Latin dance school, so it has different types of dance, but mostly salsa and bachata.

Which night was Nectar? Nectar was on Friday. How did Timba salsa? Vita, Google Maps, Instagram, someone telling you, Timba. I think I might have found Timba just by Google searching, maybe on Instagram. I think that's how I found it.

Also, how are these questions helping you to create a system? Because these are really specific to these areas.

**Notes (interviewer)**

- Timba is a Latin dance school: mostly salsa and bachata, sometimes cha-cha. Treat "Timba" as the school's name (still to confirm in the pilot).
- Nectar: Friday.
- The owner probably found Timba through a Google search, maybe Instagram (supports moves 1 and 4).
