# Strategy Library

Reusable research moves for finding a city's salsa scene. They come from the owner's interviews (`interviews/`) and the owner's own ideas. Every move is untested until a pilot scores it.

Last updated: 2026-11-16

## Moves

### 1. City events sites (Salsavida, migente.com)
- What: search "salsa <city>" and open the city guide on sites like salsavida.com, or local sites like migente.com for the DMV.
- Why: this was the owner's first way into Puerto Escondido and Cusco. It gives "solid entry points".
- Use when: always, as the first step for any city.
- Watch out: some listings aren't really for dancing (Congo, Puerto Escondido, had a "salsa hour" with about two songs). The sites have no ratings and may be out of date. Treat each listing as a lead, not a fact.
- Stop when: you've collected every listing for the city.

### 2. Google Maps reviews of salsa schools
- What: search Maps for salsa schools in the city and read their ratings and reviews.
- Why: in Cusco the owner picked a school based on its reviews.
- Use when: always. Schools are the hub of a scene.
- Stop when: you've read the top-rated schools (about 5–10) and recent reviews stop adding anything new.

### 3. Reviews that mention other places
- What: in school and venue reviews, note any other school, social, bar or park that gets named.
- Why: owner's idea. Reviews spread leads the way word of mouth does.
- Use when: reading reviews for move 2.
- Stop when: new names stop appearing.

### 4. School Instagram → events → people → more accounts
- What: find each school's Instagram and read recent posts for socials and events (day, time, price). Then follow the instructors, venues and other accounts it tags.
- Why: in Cali, the school's Instagram was the owner's best source of events. "You follow them, and you can see different events."
- Use when: you've found a school, with its Instagram usually linked from Maps or its website.
- Watch out: some posts are hard to read without logging in. Note what you couldn't reach.
- Stop when: the accounts you follow lead back to places already found.

### 5. Hostels
- What: look for hostels in the city that advertise salsa nights or classes.
- Why: in Cali the owner's hostel (La Sucursal, unverified spelling) ran beginner-friendly salsa events and passed things on by word of mouth.
- Use when: the city gets backpackers or tourists.
- Watch out: hostel events are usually social activities for complete beginners, not real socials. Label them that way.
- Stop when: you've checked the main backpacker hostels.

### 6. Reddit and forums
- What: search Reddit for "salsa <city>" and city or travel subreddits, then pull out place names and short quotes about the vibe.
- Why: owner's idea. It reads the way a human scrolling through would, and gives first-hand vibe.
- Use when: always. It's cheap, though big cities return more.
- Watch out: old threads. Record the date of every quote.
- Stop when: the threads stop naming new places.

### 7. Festivals and seasons
- What: check for salsa festivals and their dates, for example Cali's Feria around Christmas.
- Why: during the festival in Cali the owner danced in the street with non-dancers. Events change by season.
- Use when: always. Record the dates, because a scene can look very different in festival season.
- Stop when: you have the major festivals and their dates.

### 8. Group chats (WhatsApp and city chats)
- What: schools add you after you sign up for classes, and some cities have city-wide chats you hear about from people.
- Why: this was a main source of events for the owner in Cali.
- Use when: never directly. These chats are private and can't be reached from public sources. Note that a school "has a group chat" when its posts mention one, so the app can tell users.
- Stop when: n/a.

## Classifications every place needs
- Kind: social dancing / classes / live music / club with a salsa hour / hostel activity / street or park.
- Is it partner dancing? Yes or no. Salsa music alone doesn't count.
- Openness: friendly to everyone ↔ advanced or professional. This is hard to judge in advance: the owner only felt it inside ("people knew each other", "the bar was higher").
- Crowd: mostly locals ↔ mostly tourists.
- Style danced, for example Cali style vs. on1 or on2.
- Every vibe note quotes its source. Nothing is invented.

## Known blind spots
- Group chats and word-of-mouth places with forgotten names, like the intimidating venue in Cali.
- Instagram posts that need a login to read.
